Ras homolog family member A (RHOA)

This gene encodes a member of the Rho family of small GTPases, which cycle between inactive GDP-bound and active GTP-bound states and function as molecular switches in signal transduction cascades. Rho proteins promote reorganization of the actin cytoskeleton and regulate cell shape, attachment, and motility. Overexpression of this gene is associated with tumor cell proliferation and metastasis. Multiple alternatively spliced variants have been identified. [provided by RefSeq, Sep 2015].

RHOA encodes a small GTPase that cycles between GDP-bound inactive and GTP-bound active states to regulate signal transduction, actin cytoskeleton remodeling, and cell motility. It functions as a molecular switch in pathways controlling cytoskeleton organization, cell migration, and cytokinesis. RHOA activity also supports actin stress fiber assembly, focal adhesion formation, and cleavage furrow ingression.

RHOA acts through effectors such as DIAPH1 and ECT2 to drive actin stress fiber assembly and cytokinesis, and it controls microtubule stability through the MEMO1-RHOA-DIAPH1 pathway. The protein localizes to the cell cortex, cytosol, glutamatergic synapse, and plasma membrane. Expression is reported in the brain, hematopoietic system, and female reproductive system at the protein level, with RNA expression in the cardiovascular system, hematopoietic system, and connective tissue.

Pathogenic variants in RHOA are reported in ectodermal dysplasia with facial dysmorphism and acral, ocular, and brain anomalies and hemihyperplasia, isolated. RHOA is also associated with linear hypopigmentation and craniofacial asymmetry with acral, ocular and brain anomalies, T-cell non-Hodgkin lymphoma, and vascular disease, and overexpression has been linked to tumor cell proliferation and metastasis.

Aliases: ARH12, ARHA, EDFAOB, RHO12, RHOH12
