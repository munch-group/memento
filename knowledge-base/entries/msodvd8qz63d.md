---

#### PRICKLE3
- **Potential Role in Meiotic Drive**:
  - **PRICKLE3** is part of the planar cell polarity (PCP) pathway, which is essential for cellular asymmetry and cytoskeletal organization.
  - Meiotic drive systems often rely on asymmetry during gamete formation to favor one chromosome or allele over another. PRICKLE3's role in polarity suggests it could contribute to this asymmetry, especially during spindle orientation or cytokinesis in spermatocytes.
- **Supporting Evidence**:
  - PCP pathway components, including PRICKLE-family genes, are involved in spindle dynamics and flagellar assembly. Aberrant function in these pathways could lead to biased gamete formation.

---

#### HDAC6 (Histone Deacetylase 6)
- **Potential Role in Meiotic Drive**:
  - **HDAC6** plays a role in chromatin remodeling, deacetylation of histones, and regulation of microtubule dynamics. These processes are critical for proper chromosome segregation during meiosis.
  - Meiotic drive systems often exploit chromatin remodeling to enhance or suppress the segregation of specific alleles. For example, HDAC6 could deacetylate histones at specific loci to silence competing alleles or favor the segregation of selfish genetic elements.
- **Supporting Evidence**:
  - Chromatin-remodeling enzymes like HDACs have been implicated in meiotic drive in other systems by altering transcriptional or epigenetic states of selfish loci.

---

#### HUWE1 (HECT, UBA, and WWE Domain Containing E3 Ubiquitin Protein Ligase 1)  
- **Potential Role in Meiotic Drive**:
  - **HUWE1** is an E3 ubiquitin ligase involved in protein degradation, including the regulation of proteins required for meiosis (e.g., factors controlling spindle assembly, cell cycle, and germ cell differentiation).
  - In meiotic drive, HUWE1 could selectively target proteins for degradation to disrupt the normal meiotic process and promote the segregation of drive alleles.
- **Supporting Evidence**:
  - Ubiquitin-proteasome system genes are frequently implicated in meiotic drive, as they regulate key meiotic checkpoints and chromosomal segregation.

---

#### IQSEC2 (IQ Motif and Sec7 Domain 2)  
- **Potential Role in Meiotic Drive**:
  - **IQSEC2** regulates actin dynamics via Arf6 GTPase activity. While primarily studied in the nervous system, actin dynamics are equally critical in meiosis, especially for spindle positioning and cytoskeletal organization.
  - Alterations in actin dynamics could create asymmetry in gamete formation, a hallmark of meiotic drive.
- **Supporting Evidence**:
  - While direct evidence for IQSEC2 in meiotic drive is lacking, its involvement in actin regulation and cellular asymmetry makes it a potential candidate.

---

#### SRPX2 (Sushi-Repeat Containing Protein, X-Linked 2)  
- **Potential Role in Meiotic Drive**:
  - **SRPX2** is involved in cell adhesion and extracellular matrix interactions. Although less directly linked to meiotic mechanisms, its role in cellular structure could influence germ cell development and function, potentially impacting gamete selection or segregation asymmetry.
  - Meiotic drive systems occasionally exploit adhesion and extracellular signaling to bias sperm competitiveness or selection.
- **Supporting Evidence**:
  - While no direct evidence links SRPX2 to meiotic drive, extracellular matrix and adhesion proteins have been implicated in gamete competition in other systems.

---

#### SYP (Synaptophysin)  
- **Potential Role in Meiotic Drive**:
  - **SYP** is primarily involved in synaptic vesicle trafficking but also participates in vesicular transport in non-neuronal cells. While unlikely to play a direct role in meiotic drive, vesicular dynamics are critical during spermatogenesis and could indirectly influence drive systems.
- **Supporting Evidence**:
  - Vesicular transport genes are less commonly implicated in meiotic drive compared to cytoskeletal or chromatin regulators, making SYP a less likely candidate.

---

### **Strongest Candidates for Meiotic Drive**
1. **DIAPH2**: Its direct role in actin polymerization and spindle formation makes it a prime candidate for involvement in meiotic drive.
2. **PRICKLE3**: Its role in cellular asymmetry and polarity suggests potential involvement in biased segregation during meiosis.
3. **HDAC6**: Its function in chromatin remodeling and microtubule regulation aligns with mechanisms often exploited by meiotic drive systems.
4. **HUWE1**: As a regulator of protein degradation, it could modulate key meiotic factors, influencing segregation asymmetry.

### **Weaker Candidates**
- **IQSEC2**: Possible involvement due to actin dynamics but lacks direct evidence.
- **SRPX2 and SYP**: Likely have limited or indirect roles in meiotic drive.

---

### Conclusion
The strongest candidates for involvement in **meiotic drive** are **DIAPH2, PRICKLE3, HDAC6,** and **HUWE1**, based on their roles in cytoskeletal dynamics, chromatin remodeling, and protein regulation, all of which are commonly exploited by meiotic drive systems. Further functional studies or population genetic evidence would help confirm their roles.

As we discussed, it is pretty amazing that the even X/Y transmission in spermatogenesis works at all if all an X (or Y) gene, essential to late spermatids/spermatozoa, needs to do is delay its expression until it can no longer be shared with cells with the Y (or X), instead of expressing early and handing it to the Y (or X) it through the chromatoid body.
