A loss-of-function (LoF) mutation within the core NuRD complex itself would likely suppress expansion rather than cause it. Genetic screens demonstrate that catalytic components like CHD4 act as critical activators of terminal recombination; losing them blocks the architectural remodeling needed to copy and expand these regions. This critical recruitment process is documented in Nature Structural & Molecular Biology: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25150861/ However, an LoF phenotype can absolutely explain subtelomeric expansion if the target is a master epigenetic suppressor that normally keeps terminal heterochromatin locked down. When these protective "brakes" are rendered non-functional, the locus relaxes, triggering runaway homologous recombination that rapidly drives up the size and copy number of the region.

https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1084952123002276


The most viable candidates for an evolutionary or mechanistic LoF expansion model include:


# 1. ATRX / DAXX Loss of Function (The Classic Recombination Trigger)

The chromatin remodeler ATRX and its histone chaperone partner DAXX are responsible for depositing the specific histone variant H3.3 to keep highly repetitive telomeric and subtelomeric DNA tightly packed, organized, and silent.

The LoF Mechanism: In human biology, the functional loss of ATRX or DAXX is a classic molecular driver behind Alternative Lengthening of Telomeres (ALT). Without them, replication forks stall on repetitive sequences, creating double-strand breaks that recruit repair machineries to copy and expand the adjacent arrays aggressively. This phenotype can be explored in these research papers:

https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12564824/
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3202281/

- Evolutionary Parallel: If an ancestral lineage (such as the branch leading to gorillas or siamangs) experienced a germline attenuation or a regulatory "dialing down" of ATRX-mediated silencing, it would create a highly permissive environment for massive subtelomeric duplication.

# 2. Loss of Chromatin Assembly Chaperones (HIRA, ASF1A, CHAF1B)

High-throughput CRISPR-knockout screens mapping terminal sequence regulators have systematically identified a dedicated class of ALT Suppressors.

- The LoF Mechanism: Histone chaperones like ASF1A, HIRA, and CHAF1B manage replication-dependent and independent nucleosome assembly pathways. Their primary task is to quickly "smother" newly synthesized or open repetitive DNA with nucleosomes to prevent hyper-recombination.
-• The Phenotype: Knockout or structural loss-of-function of these specific chaperones immediately elevates open, single-stranded DNA at chromosome terminals. This forces the cell to expand the locus via template-switching and break-induced replication (BIR) mechanisms. Papers studying these chaperone dynamics can be found here:

https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3946341/
https://www.cell.com/molecular-cell/fulltext/S1097-2765(26)00426-0


# 3. Epigenetic Erasure (SUV39H1/2 or DNMTs)

Subtelomeric structural stability relies entirely on maintaining dense protective marks, specifically DNA methylation and histone H3K9me3 methylation.

https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1084952123002276

- The LoF Mechanism: If the histone methyltransferases SUV39H1/SUV39H2 or the maintenance DNA methyltransferase DNMT1 suffer a loss-of-function, the subtelomeres enter a state of severe hypomethylation.
- The Phenotype: This extreme chromatin relaxation causes a surge of long non-coding TERRA transcripts. Overexpressed TERRA loops back onto the DNA template, forming disruptive R-loops that shatter the replication fork and force the machinery to rearrange and expand the adjacent subtelomeric blocks.

Summary of the Primate Model

To view gorilla or siamang expansion through a NuRD-centric lens, the phenotype would require a loss-of-function of a NuRD inhibitor—a protein that normally blocks ZNF827 or NuRD from accessing chromosome ends. Otherwise, the simpler evolutionary model points to an LoF or regulatory dampening of ATRX or its associated histone chaperones, which effectively takes the brakes off terminal sequence stability. ?
