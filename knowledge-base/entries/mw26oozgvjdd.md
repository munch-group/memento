The relevant constraints come from one key paper. In live rat spermatids, granules move continuously into the bridge but only 28% of those entering actually crossed, and average granule speed fell significantly during passage; the chromatoid body (~1.8 µm) traverses the bridge; and microtubule inhibitors abolished all movement through bridges and disintegrated the chromatoid body (PMID 12857863, doi:10.1091/mbc.e02-10-0647). So there is already a stalling/filtering step with a <1/3 pass rate, and it is microtubule-motor dependent — exactly the kind of step a reversible PTM can bias.

Sharing is real (a post-meiotic transgene product is shared between genetically distinct spermatids: PMID 2911388, doi:10.1038/337373a0) but demonstrably incomplete and cargo-specific: Spam1 mRNA and protein are compartmentalised and not shared, producing biochemically different sperm and transmission ratio distortion (PMID 11369602, doi:10.1095/biolreprod64.6.1730). Single-cell sequencing of Tex14 hypomorphs confirms transcript sharing between wild-type spermatids and its partial loss when bridges are reduced (PMID 39929837, doi:10.1038/s41467-025-56742-9). The bridge itself is a converted midbody — TEX14 locks centralspindlin (MKLP1/KIF23, MgcRacGAP) in place, with septins 2/7/9 transiting (PMID 17383626, doi:10.1016/j.ydbio.2007.02.025).

That gives four control points: a barrier in the aperture, the motor/adaptor that pulls cargo through, the material state of the cargo, and any asymmetry between the two connected cells.

1. Gating — O-GlcNAc sets the stringency of disordered-protein permeability barriers
The strongest mechanistic precedent is the nuclear pore. FG-repeat domains are the major O-GlcNAc site in the NPC; raising NPC O-GlcNAc accelerates receptor-facilitated transport and also the passive permeation of a small inert protein, lowering it slows transport, and super-resolution puts O-GlcNAc right at the FG barrier (PMID 33909044, doi:10.1083/jcb.202010141). Separately, loss of O-GlcNAc destabilises nucleoporins, exposes them to ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation, and degrades the selectivity filter itself (PMID 26031751, doi:10.1093/jmcb/mjv033).

Two caveats matter. It is not established that the spermatid bridge contains an FG-type hydrogel barrier — that is the weak link in this chain. And the NPC effect was explicitly bidirectional, so this mechanism tunes how permissive the aperture is (the size/avidity cutoff that makes transport selective), not which way cargo goes.

2. Motors and adaptors — O-GlcNAc as a vectorial brake
OGT O-GlcNAcylates the motor-adaptor Milton/TRAK at mapped serines and thereby arrests mitochondrial motility, with the modification state set by extracellular glucose (PMID 24995978, doi:10.1016/j.cell.2014.06.007). This is the cleanest precedent for "OGT activity decides whether a cargo keeps moving."

Applied to the bridge: transport through it is microtubule-dependent, and the germ-cell cargo carriers are known — KIF17b shuttles MIWI and chromatoid-body material (PMID 16787948, doi:10.1242/jcs.03022), and TB-RBP/Translin moves mRNPs through bridges (PMID 9733578, doi:10.1006/dbio.1998.8967). If the microtubule array spanning a bridge has a polarity bias, then O-GlcNAc-dependent inactivation of a plus-end- versus minus-end-directed adaptor is intrinsically directional: it converts bidirectional shuttling into net flux. No O-GlcNAc site has been reported on KIF17b or TB-RBP, so this is extrapolation from the Milton paradigm, not a documented link.

3. Cargo side — condensate material state as a size filter
The cargo that crosses is largely RNP granules, and O-GlcNAc is an established switch on condensate behaviour: it controls YTHDF1/3 translation activity and the assembly, stability and disassembly of stress granules (PMID 37945829, doi:10.1038/s41556-023-01258-x), and engineered catalytically dead OGT can be used to retarget stress-granule regulation (PMID 41501012, doi:10.1038/s41467-025-66689-6). OGT/OGA tone could therefore decide whether a given mRNP stays a small, deformable, mobile granule that clears a ~1 µm aperture, or coarsens into a retained condensate. That is selectivity by cargo identity rather than by the bridge, and it fits the Spam1 case, where the message is compartmentalised and translated on the spot rather than packaged for travel.

4. Where direction could come from
Directional transport needs a symmetry break between the two connected cells. Two candidates specifically involve these genes.

Metabolic. OGT activity tracks UDP-GlcNAc from the hexosamine pathway, i.e. glucose, glutamine, acetyl-CoA and UTP supply. Spermatids within one clone are not equidistant from Sertoli-cell lactate/glucose delivery, so a hexosamine gradient across a syncytium would produce an O-GlcNAc gradient and therefore a gradient in whichever switch above is operative — net flux down the gradient. The Milton work is exactly this logic (glucose → OGT → transport arrest), so the mechanism form is already validated elsewhere.

Genetic, and this is the sharpest prediction. Human OGT is X-linked at Xq13 adjacent to the X-inactivation centre and is dosage-sensitive (PMID 24960196, doi:10.1016/j.bbrc.2014.06.068; PMID 25136351, doi:10.3389/fgene.2014.00256), whereas OGA/MGEA5 is autosomal. After meiosis the sex chromosomes are packaged into post-meiotic sex chromatin and largely silenced (PMID 16809775, doi:10.1128/MCB.00519-06; PMID 27795737, doi:10.1186/s13072-016-0099-8), so post-meiotic OGT output is limited in every spermatid — but any residual X-linked expression or unequal partitioning of pre-meiotic OGT protein at the meiotic divisions would make OGT differ between X- and Y-bearing spermatids while OGA does not. The consequence is a feedback loop: the enzyme that tunes the sharing machinery is itself one of the X-linked products that sharing exists to equalise. Incomplete sharing of OGT would give X-bearing spermatids higher O-GlcNAc tone, bias transport in their favour, and reinforce the asymmetry — a candidate route to transmission ratio distortion of the Spam1 type. Speculative, but directly testable.

5. Architectural and confounding effects you cannot ignore
O-GlcNAc–phosphorylation crosstalk is pervasive on the spindle and midbody machinery, with 141 O-GlcNAc sites mapped on cytokinesis proteins, many at or adjacent to phosphosites (PMID 20068230, doi:10.1126/scisignal.2000526). Since the bridge is a stabilised midbody built on MKLP1/MgcRacGAP, OGT/OGA could set bridge diameter, stability or composition outright — changing the size cutoff rather than the transport kinetics.

The practical confound: germ-cell Ogt deletion (Stra8-Cre) blocks spermatogonial differentiation and meiotic progression via HCFC1, SRSF1 and SF3B3, causing infertility (PMID 42399815, doi:10.1186/s11658-026-00972-2), and raising O-GlcNAc by OGA inhibition with Thiamet-G causes pachytene arrest through synapsis and recombination defects (PMID 37188682, doi:10.1038/s41420-023-01433-x). Most perturbations will therefore destroy the cells before round spermatids exist, so any bridge phenotype needs acute or post-meiotic manipulation to be interpretable.
